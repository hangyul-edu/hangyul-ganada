# Notes for the Play reviewer

Pasted into Play Console › App content › (the notes field on the release).

---

Hangyul ganada teaches an adult beginner to read and write Korean, starting from
the first letter of the alphabet.

**No account is needed.** There is no sign-in, no sign-up and no server. Every
screen is reachable from a fresh install with no credential of any kind, so there
is no demo login to provide.

**It works offline.** Every lesson, recording and typeface is inside the binary.
Turning aeroplane mode on before opening the app is a fair test; the correct
result is that nothing changes. The app makes no network request at runtime.

**The five-minute path.** Open the app, tap the primary button on the home
screen, and follow it. Within three screens you will hear a Korean letter, watch
it being written stroke by stroke, and trace it on the canvas with a finger. That
is the product.

**One permission is requested, and only if you ask for it.** Settings has an
optional daily reminder that is off by default. Switching it on and choosing a
time is the only thing in the app that produces a system permission prompt.
Nothing else asks for anything.

**Handwriting is graded on the device.** What you draw is compared against the
outline of the letter in the selected typeface by a geometric evaluator that runs
locally. Nothing is uploaded, and the drawing is discarded once it is scored.

**The audio.** 5,275 utterances in two Korean voices, generated ahead of time and
packaged with the app. Every speaker button works with the network off.

**What it is not**: not a TOPIK course, not a chatbot, not a subscription, not a
social app. It is bought once.
