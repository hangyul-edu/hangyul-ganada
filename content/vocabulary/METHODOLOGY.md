# The vocabulary corpus — how it was built and what was cut

This document exists because a paid product should be able to answer "why is
this word in here, and why is that one not?" without anyone having to read
Python.

## Where the words come from

The candidate list is the Korean section of English Wiktionary (CC BY-SA 4.0),
which supplies **only** two things: which Korean words exist, and each one's
part of speech. Nothing else in the shipped dataset comes from it unedited.

Every candidate is then reviewed by hand and written into an editorial pack at
`content/vocabulary/entries/*.jsonl`, one record per word. A word that has not
been reviewed does not ship. A word that has been reviewed and rejected does not
ship either, and carries the reason it was cut in the same file. That inversion
is the point: the dataset is a reviewed corpus, not an import with a filter over
it.

## What each reviewed record carries

| Field | What it is |
| --- | --- |
| `m` | The meaning in Korean, Japanese, Chinese, Spanish, French, German and Portuguese, written for the card rather than translated from the English gloss |
| `ex` | A Korean example sentence written for a beginner |
| `t` | That sentence in the seven other shipping languages |
| `v` | The illustration spec, which is also the concreteness signal the difficulty model reads |
| `u` | Learner usefulness, 1 (needed in week one) to 5 (advanced) |
| `en`, `pos` | Corrections to the dictionary's English gloss or part of speech, where it was wrong |

## The three numbers, and why they are three

* **Frequency** is measured, not estimated. Two open OpenSubtitles Korean
  corpora (2018, 688,129 tokens; 2016, 299,195) are read in full, and each
  word's conjugated surface forms are counted against them. The result is a rate
  per million, a rank, and a band. A word the corpora never saw is recorded as
  `observed: false` and is given no rank and no rate — not a mid-range default.
* **Difficulty** is computed by `scripts/content/difficulty.py` from nine
  declared features with declared weights, binned into eight levels of roughly
  equal size. It answers *how hard is this word to learn*.
* **Letter readiness** is computed by `scripts/content/readiness.py` and answers
  *can I write this with the letters I have met*. It is a note on the card. It
  gates nothing.

The previous version of this dataset had one number doing all three jobs, and it
was derived from the letters a word contained. That is why 맛있다 — a word every
learner meets in their first week — was filed at level 10, alongside 몰아넣다.

### The known limit of the frequency measurement

The corpora are not lemmatised, so a verb whose stem is spelled like a common
noun can absorb some of that noun's count. Two guards are in place: a verb stem
is only counted with a genuinely verbal ending attached, and a token that is
itself another headword in the dataset is credited to that headword. What
remains is a small over-count on a handful of rare verbs with one-syllable
stems. It is bounded, it is in one direction, and it is written down here rather
than smoothed away.

## Why words were removed

Every removal carries its reason in the pack. The categories, with examples:

| Reason | Examples |
| --- | --- |
| **Not a headword** — an inflected or nominalised form the importer mistook for a word | 와라, 지난, 아니야, 미친, 좋아요, 말하기, 있음, 먹기, 만나기 |
| **A word plus a particle** | 나랑, 일이, 저의, 자의, 적의, 이의, 지구의, 등에, 사람들, 아이들, 모두들 |
| **Dialect or archaic**, not the standard Korean the curriculum teaches | 카다, 쿠다, 아이가, 거시기, 마실, 헤다, 외다, 안해, 마스다 |
| **Adult or crude**, unsuitable in a beginner product | 섹스, 창녀, 개새끼, 처먹다, 떡치다, 보지, 자지, 병신, 멍청이 |
| **Sensitive subject matter** outside an alphabet course's scope | 자살, 살인하다, 납치, 고문하다, 사형, 테러, 코카인 |
| **Homograph the card cannot resolve** — two unrelated words, one spelling | 주지, 수사, 하자, 한대, 내지, 지면, 성하다, 파이, 나이트 |
| **Too specialised** for everyday Korean | 초음파, 이식, 종양, 흉부, 복부, 두개골, 메스, 클램프, 원치, 해리, 피트, 마일 |
| **Duplicate of a word already taught** | 시티 (도시), 베이비 (아기), 박스 (상자), 풋볼 (축구), 살해 (살인) |
| **Wrong gloss with no recoverable sense** | 밀어, 비의, 전하, 대한, 하면, 석다, 씨다, 디다, 하이다, 지루다 |
| **A name or title rather than vocabulary** | 나치, 가나, 성하, 각하, 폐하, 보안관, 사람인 |
| **Military rank or unit-specific** | 대위, 소령, 대령, 중위 |
| **Race words**, with no pedagogical need in this scope | 흑인, 백인 |

Nothing was removed to make a coverage percentage easier to reach. The
percentages are computed over whatever the corpus ends up being, and a removal
that made a row easier to fill would still have had to carry a reason a reviewer
would accept.

## Illustrations

Every drawing is original work, composed by `scripts/content/illustration/` from
a library of house-style parts. Nothing is downloaded, matched against a stock
set, or generated by a third-party model, so there is one visual style across
the whole corpus and one licence to declare. Concrete nouns get the object;
verbs get a figure doing the thing; adjectives get a comparison; abstract words
get a purpose-drawn diagram. Words with genuinely the same meaning may share a
spec — 기쁘다 and 즐겁다 are both a laughing face, because they both mean that —
but the spec is chosen per word and the file is written per word.

## Audio

Both voices, every word and every sentence, generated at build time and bundled.
The commercial licensing position for the shipped clips is recorded in
`docs/AUDIO.md`; regenerating under a paid subscription is the one step of this
that needs a credential the repository does not have.
